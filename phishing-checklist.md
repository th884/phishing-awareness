# Phishing Quick Checklist

Use this checklist when an unexpected message asks you to act. If unsure, stop and verify through a trusted channel.

## Before interactin

-[] Were you expecting this message or does it seem out of place?
-[] Does it create a sense of urgency, fear, excitement or secrecy?
-[] Does it ask you for a password, verification code, payment, personal information or to change your account?
-[] Is there anything unexpected about the sender’s email address, phone number or account name? Perhaps they have a slight spelling mistake.
-[] Is there an unexpected link or attachment? Or maybe they are asking you to log-in or turn on something?
-[] Can you check this request by using a known website/number/contact?

## If anything seems suspicious

Do not do any of the following:

-[] Click, open, respond to, scan or enter information.
-[] Report the message through your organisation’s approved security reporting system or the reporting option within the service.
-[] Follow instructions provided locally for handling the message and evidence.
-[] Delete or isolate the message.

## If I already interacted

[ ] Reach out to your IT/security team using a reliable communication method
[ ] If you’ve entered a password, change it via the legitimate service and make sure you’re not using the same password elsewhere.
[ ] If you’ve provided a code, authorised a suspicious login or transferred money, contact the relevant organisation or service provider immediately.
[ ] Save any relevant evidence following the guidelines.

A message may look legitimate due to a realistic looking logo and well written language. Always verify any unusual requests (NIST, n.d., FTC, n.d.).
